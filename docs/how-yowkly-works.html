How Yowkly works

A plain-English guide to the whole system: what the pieces are, what each one does, and what actually happens when someone signs up, uploads a template, votes, posts or chats. No coding knowledge needed.

Version of October 2026 · covers everything built so far

1. The big picture

Yowkly is a website where developers and designers share templates, app designs and website showcases, vote for the best ones with yowkbits, and talk to each other in a community with posts, channels and chat.

Behind the website are a handful of online services that each do one job. Think of Yowkly as a shop in a mall:

The shop front

Vercel

Hosts the website (yowkly.vercel.app). It builds the pages people see and runs the small "server actions" that happen when someone clicks a button, like voting or posting. Every time new code is pushed to GitHub, Vercel rebuilds the site by itself.

The vault and the security guard

Supabase

Holds all the information: accounts, templates, votes, posts, chats, channels. It also handles logins (email and Google) and the live updates that make chat instant. Most importantly, it stores the rules for who may see and change what.

The warehouse

Cloudflare R2

Stores the actual files: template downloads, cover images, post photos and chat photos. Three areas: a quarantine for new uploads, a private area for scanned files and chat photos, and a public area for images everyone can see.

The inspection room

GitHub Actions

Runs the virus scanner (ClamAV). When someone uploads a template file, the scanner checks it before anyone else can download it. Infected or broken files are rejected and deleted.

The turnstile counter

Upstash

Counts how often each person does things (sign-in attempts, posts, likes) so nobody can hammer the site. If someone goes too fast, they're asked to slow down.

Where the code lives

GitHub

Keeps every version of the code and the database changes ("migrations"). Pushing to GitHub triggers Vercel to update the live site.

The one idea that matters most: the website never decides on its own whether something is allowed. Every rule ("only the owner can edit this", "you can't vote on your own template", "only friends can chat freely") is written into the database. So even if someone tampers with the website in their browser, the database still says no. This is called row-level security.

2. What happens when…

…someone signs up

  1. They enter an email and password (or press Google). Supabase creates the account and gives them 100 yowkbits.
  2. An email arrives with a Confirm my email button. It opens a page on Yowkly; one click confirms the account. (The button page protects against email scanners using up the link.)
  3. They pick a few interests, which shape their For You feed. They can change these later in Settings.

…someone uploads a template

  1. Basics: title, tagline, description, category and at least one tag (all required).
  2. Source: files (ZIP, Figma, PSD and others), links (GitHub, Figma, Canva…) and an optional live demo. The demo must be the creator's own site; Google, Facebook, YouTube and link shorteners are refused.
  3. Files go straight from the browser into the quarantine area. The scanner checks them within minutes. Clean files move to the private area; bad ones are deleted and the creator is told why.
  4. Media: a cover image and gallery, each cropped in the browser and shrunk before upload so pages stay fast.
  5. License and version, then Publish. A brand-new creator's first two uploads wait for a moderator's approval; after that, they publish straight away.

Inspo works the same way but is a showcase: no files or download links at all, just screenshots and a "Visit the live site" button.

…someone downloads

They must be signed in and accept the template's license. The site then hands them a download link that works for only one minute, so links can't be shared around. Each download is recorded once a day per person.

…someone votes

A vote costs 1 yowkbit, one vote per template, and votes are final. You can't vote on your own work, and accounts newer than 24 hours can't vote (stops throwaway accounts). Votes decide the Ranking: Template of the Day, Week and Month, and Dev of the Week. Likes are separate and free; they don't affect the Ranking.

3. The community

Posts. Anyone signed in can post text, up to 4 photos and up to 5 tags. Others can like or dislike (one each), comment, and like or dislike comments. The best one or two comments show under each post. Authors can edit or delete their own posts from the ⋮ menu.

Channels. Topic spaces anyone can create (up to 5). The creator is the owner and can make members moderators. Owners and moderators can remove posts, kick people, and ban them with a reason. Every action goes into the channel's own mod log. You must join a channel to post in it.

Friends. A friend is someone you follow who follows you back. Your friends list is private unless you make it public in Settings.

Activity status. Online, Idle or Appear offline, chosen from your account menu. The site also notices if you've gone quiet (idle after 10 minutes, offline after 3 minutes away). Only friends ever see your status.

Chat. Friends can message each other freely. Anyone else lands in your Requests; they can send up to 3 messages until you accept, decline or block. Messages arrive instantly, with typing dots, from the Messages page or a floating chat box. Chat photos are kept private and shown through links that expire after an hour.

Notifications. Follows, comments, approvals, rankings and scan results appear in the bell popup, with the person's profile picture.

4. Keeping it safe

RiskWhat stops it
Someone edits another person's template, post or chatThe database only lets owners change their own things (row-level security), no matter what the browser sends.
Viruses in uploaded filesEvery file waits in quarantine until the virus scanner clears it. Nobody can download an unscanned file.
Spam and abuseSpeed limits (Upstash) plus daily caps in the database: posts, comments, messages, requests and photos. Message requests limit strangers to 3 messages.
Harmful contentAnyone can Report a template, post, comment or message. Moderators see reports in the Admin panel and can remove content. Admins can suspend accounts.
Fake "images" that are really codeChat photos are checked byte by byte on the server; SVG images (which can carry code) are refused everywhere.
Private photos leakingChat photos live in the private storage area and are only shown through links that expire, and only to the two people in the chat.
Rewriting historyPublished template versions, votes and audit logs can't be edited. Deleted posts are hidden, not erased, so reports can still be judged.

Who can do what

RoleCan do
MemberUpload, vote, post, comment, chat, create channels.
Sub-adminApprove uploads or ask for changes. Chosen by an admin from their friends.
ModeratorEverything a sub-admin can, plus handle reports and remove content anywhere.
AdminEverything, plus the user directory, making moderators, trusting creators (skip review) and suspending accounts. Every admin action is logged.

5. Speed

  • Caching: public pages (templates, profiles, the feed) are saved and reused for up to a minute, so most visits never touch the database. When something changes, that page is refreshed straight away for its owner.
  • Small images: every image is cropped and shrunk in the person's own browser before it's uploaded. A 6 MB photo usually becomes 100–300 KB.
  • Only load what's needed: the live-chat code (the heaviest part) only loads when a chat is actually opened.

7. Words you'll hear

Database: the organised store of all the site's information.

Migration: a saved change to the database's structure (a new table, a new rule). Applied with db push.

Row-level security (RLS): rules inside the database deciding who can see or change each row. The main safety net.

Server action: a small piece of code that runs on Vercel when someone clicks a button.

Realtime: the Supabase feature that pushes new chat messages to the screen instantly.

Bucket: a storage area in R2 (quarantine, private files, public media).

Cache: a saved copy of a page reused for a short time so the site stays fast.

Rate limit: a cap on how often someone can do something.

Yowkbits: the voting currency. Everyone starts with 100; one vote costs one.

Environment variables: the site's private settings (keys, addresses), kept in Vercel and GitHub, not in the code.
